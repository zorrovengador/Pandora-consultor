# Grill Me

Use this skill when the user asks to pressure-test an idea, business, proposal, project, workflow, product, strategy, or decision. The goal is decision quality, not interrogation theater.

## Procedure

1. Ask exactly one high-value question at a time.
2. Prefer questions with the highest information gain and upstream dependencies.
3. Briefly reflect the implication of the answer, then expose the next unresolved dependency.
4. Convert vague language into concrete users, buyers, behaviors, numbers, dates, owners, channels, constraints, and evidence.
5. Separate facts, user decisions, assumptions, risks, and recommendations.
6. Research externally verifiable claims instead of silently treating them as assumptions.
7. Make trade-offs explicit and identify what evidence would change the decision.
8. End with one of four outcomes: proceed, revise, park, or discard.
9. Define a cheap behavioral experiment or falsification test whenever it can reduce uncertainty.

## Business and strategy probes

Test pain and current workaround; target user, buyer and owner; urgency and timing; alternatives; differentiation; acquisition channel; pricing logic; unit economics; dependencies; failure modes; reversibility; success criteria; and the smallest experiment that can produce behavioral evidence.

Do not accept “para empresas”, “para todos”, “barato”, “viral” or “lo vemos después” without narrowing the statement.

## Plan and project probes

Clarify intended outcome, definition of done, beneficiary, decision-maker, current state, scope and exclusions, resources, timing, dependencies, risks, approval boundaries, acceptance criteria and stop/escalation condition.

## Response format

Keep each turn compact:

1. One-sentence implication or challenge.
2. Optional provisional recommendation, clearly labeled.
3. Exactly one question.

## Finish deliverable

When the shared understanding is sufficient, provide:

- problem and desired outcome;
- target user/buyer and context;
- current alternative;
- proposed solution or decision;
- evidence and sources;
- assumptions and open questions;
- risks and approval boundaries;
- first experiment or next action;
- success and stop criteria;
- decision: proceed, revise, park, or discard.

## Safety and verification

Do not publish, contact people, spend money, change production, schedule work, or modify external systems merely because the interview identified such actions. Ask for separate authorization. Before closing, verify that facts are sourced or labeled unknown, recommendations are not presented as decisions, vague claims became testable, and no external side effect occurred without approval.
